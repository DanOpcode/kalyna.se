## Who keeps the technical work on track?

In a small product team, the same people often build the next release, support their teammates and decide how the system should evolve. When the next release takes up all their time, bigger technical questions can get put off. Meanwhile, the team has to keep building before those decisions are settled.

I join your team and take responsibility for working through those questions and making sure decisions are made. Together we work out what to tackle now, what can wait and how to break up the work. Then I help build it. I can work wherever the team needs me, from architecture and development to testing and deployment.

## Architecture your team can build on

When I design an architecture, I start with what the system needs to do and how the team will work with it. Which parts does the team need to be able to change independently? What do we actually need to build now? My experience in full-stack development and DevOps helps me make those trade-offs and then help build the solution.

I also use AI agents for clearly scoped tasks, such as mapping dependencies, building a prototype or writing tests. I review the results based on how they fit into the system and what colleagues will need to understand and change later.

## About Kalyna

“Kalyna” is the Ukrainian word “Калина” written in Latin letters. It is a plant known in English as [guelder rose](<https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skogsolvon>). It is a traditional Ukrainian symbol whose berries represent the homeland. In Ukraine, the guelder rose appears in songs, art, embroidery and poetry. One now well-known song is “[Oh, the Red Viburnum in the Meadow](<https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ett_r%C3%B6tt_olvon_vid_%C3%A4ngen>)”, written in 1914. Its original title is “Ой у лузі червона калина”, and it is [still sung today](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EV_vT0Vud5Q>).
